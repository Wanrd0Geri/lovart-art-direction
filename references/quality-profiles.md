# Quality Profiles

Translate quality into visible behavior.
Require medium-appropriate nonuniform variation instead of generic polish.

For physical materials, vary roughness, texture scale, reflection, transmission, seams, or manufacturing response in ways supported by the object's state.
For 2D media, vary strokes, edges, grain, value grouping, or color shapes.
For VFX, vary density, turbulence, edge breakup, particle decay, and phase behavior.
Use wear, oxidation, patina, or fading only when age, use, and storage support it.
Do not age pristine, ceremonial, sterile, or intentionally flat assets.

## Cinematic realistic environment or key visual

- Define the medium and rendering logic.
- Use motivated key, fill, bounce, and practical light relationships.
- Describe state-appropriate material response and surface variation.
- Establish clear value groups and focal hierarchy.
- Grade detail density from focal structures to supporting regions.
- Tie atmospheric depth to scale and weather.
- Use a credible lens position and spatial composition.

Keep the visual system stable across a set without forcing identical lenses, weather, or camera positions.

## Realistic character or creature key visual

- Establish identity and silhouette before ornament.
- Describe skin, fur, scale, cloth, armor, or other appropriate surface response.
- Keep costume hierarchy and equipment placement legible.
- Preserve contact with the ground, clothing, and environment.
- Separate the subject through value, color, depth, or light rather than automatic rim light.
- Match camera and expression to the intended use.

Do not automatically add shallow depth of field, strong rim light, film grain, pores, or dramatic grading.

## Neutral design sheet

- Keep proportions, eye height, lighting, and background comparable.
- Show complete silhouettes and unobstructed views.
- Make construction, seams, attachments, and material boundaries readable.
- Add nonuniform craft or surface response only when it aids construction readability.

Avoid dramatic shadow, extreme perspective, shallow depth of field, environmental occlusion, and pose exaggeration.

## 2D illustration or digital matte painting

- Define medium behavior rather than borrowing an artist name.
- Specify edge hierarchy, shape language, value groups, and color relationships.
- Use stroke, grain, flatness, or texture variation appropriate to the medium.
- Express material distinction through painting language.
- Use spatial depth and detail density to support the focal area.

Do not force camera brands, realistic skin terminology, film stock, or PBR language.

## Scene-integrated VFX

- Define a physical or magical source and readable structural hierarchy.
- Specify trajectory, volume, occlusion, and scale.
- Add illumination on nearby subjects and surfaces.
- Add relevant smoke, debris, ground contact, or air disturbance.
- Vary density, edge breakup, and dissipation residue.

Make the effect belong to the scene rather than sit over it as a glowing layer.

## Isolated staged VFX sheet

- Lock subject position, camera, background, and scale reference.
- Label stages and show causal state changes.
- Explain energy growth, transfer, peak, and decay.
- Evolve the core, body, edge, turbulence, particles, and residue.

Prioritize stage comparability over cinematic shot variety.
